### Who (Subject Identification)

<!-- Subject name/entity, account numbers, role, relationship to institution -->
This case involves fifteen structurally independent transaction chains, each comprising a distinct originating account, a single intermediary account, and a distinct destination account, spanning 45 unique accounts held by a mix of corporations, partnerships, and sole proprietorships. No account functions in more than one chain. Two chains, however, share a common institution via different account numbers: National Bank of the East (Bank ID 12) serves as the destination in one chain (account 80C5C01D0) and the origination point of a separate chain (account 800EEECD0); Italy Bank #95 (Bank ID 1490) serves as the intermediary in one chain (account 80BB81D50) and the destination of a separate chain (account 80B9BD900).

### What (Suspicious Activity)

<!-- Transaction types, amounts, instruments used -->
Between September 7 and September 11, 2022, fifteen parallel two-hop transaction chains moved funds from an originating account, through a single intermediary account, to a final destination account, with each chain operating independently of the others. Individual transaction amounts ranged from $98.06 to $1,632,281.02 (Yen-denominated), with each chain converting currency at least once — nine currencies appear across the case in total (Australian Dollar, Euro, Mexican Peso, Rupee, Swiss Franc, UK Pound, US Dollar, Yen, Yuan).

### Why Suspicious

<!-- Explanation of why the activity is unusual, typology match -->
Fifteen structurally identical two-hop chains — each an isolated originator-intermediary-destination sequence with no shared accounts — operating in parallel over the same 92.8-hour window is inconsistent with fifteen unrelated, coincidentally similar transaction patterns. This structural uniformity, combined with two direct institutional overlaps between nominally independent chains (National Bank of the East and Italy Bank #95 each appearing as both an endpoint in one chain and a different role in another, via different account numbers), suggests common coordination across chains that would otherwise appear unconnected at the account level. A currency-conversion consistency check reinforces this: two separate chains (originating 09/07 and 09/08) both convert Yuan to US Dollar at implied rates of 0.1424 and 0.1468 — a spread of roughly 3% between supposedly unrelated conversions occurring one day apart, tight enough to suggest a common exchange-rate source rather than independent, arms-length conversion. The consistent use of a single intermediary account per chain, each severing the trail after exactly one hop, matches a layering technique in which value is passed through a chain of accounts to obscure the connection between origin and destination, adapted here to traditional bank transfers rather than the virtual-currency e-wallet chains described in the cited source (see FATF, Professional Money Laundering (2018), Box 6). Multiple customers transacting internationally in the same week is not inherently suspicious, but the combination of uniform two-hop structure, shared institutions across chains, and matching implied exchange rates is not adequately explained by coincidence.

### How (Method / Mechanism)

<!-- How the activity was conducted — structuring method, layering technique -->
Each of the fifteen chains followed an identical structure: an originating account transferred funds to a single intermediary account, which then forwarded the funds — after a currency conversion in all but one instance — to a final destination account, severing any further traceable connection. No intermediary account received more than one inbound transfer or sent more than one outbound transfer, and the fifteen chains operated in parallel over the same window rather than sequentially.
